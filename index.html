    How it works, and its limits

    Model. Logistic regression on 20 pre-match features: Elo and Glicko-2 rating gaps, recent form over 5 and 10 matches, experience, career record, rest days and head-to-head. Trained on 17,193 matches from 2022–2024.

    Order symmetry. The model scores each fixture both ways round and averages in log-odds, so P(A beats B) = 1 − P(B beats A) exactly. Context that is the same for both sides (tournament tier, round) cancels out, which is why the model is a short weighted sum that this page can explain factor by factor.

    No leakage. Every match on a given date is rated only from matches before that date.

    • Ratings are frozen at 9 August 2026, the end of the scraped data. Results after that date are not reflected.
    • Men's singles is the hardest discipline to call (ROC-AUC about 0.73). Women's doubles is the easiest (about 0.86).
    • The 0.80 ROC-AUC target was met in 2025 (0.808) but not in 2026 (0.798). Both intervals contain 0.80.
    • Probabilities are uncalibrated, and 2026 calibration is weaker than 2025 (ECE 0.038 against 0.014).
    • The home and fatigue adjustments are measured effects. The combined adjustment did not clearly beat a simple recalibration on 2026, so treat them as context.
    • A doubles pair the data has never seen together starts from a default partnership rating.
